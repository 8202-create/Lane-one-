Built for the run you just finished, not the one you're planning to track.

Lane One started with a simple question: why does checking your pace need an app download, a login screen, and access to your location? It shouldn't. So this doesn't ask for any of that.

The idea

Every running app wants to be your permanent training log. Lane One isn't trying to be that. It's for the moment right after a run, when you just want to know: how fast was that, really, and was it a good effort? You type in two numbers, and it gives you a real answer — grounded in the same formulas coaches and exercise scientists actually use — without asking you to hand over an account first.

What it stands for

No accounts, ever

Nothing is stored, nothing is synced. Refresh the page and your run is gone — that's intentional, not a missing feature.

Honest estimates

Calorie counts, effort zones, and comparisons are clearly labelled as estimates based on standard formulas — not personalized medical or training advice.

Analysis you can actually read

Instead of a wall of charts, Lane One writes your results back to you in plain language — like a coach glancing at your watch and telling you what it means.
